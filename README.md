## Project Background

Tuberculosis (TB) remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide from a single infectious agent. It is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (Koch's bacillus) and is primarily transmitted through airborne droplets released by individuals with active pulmonary disease.

In Brazil, tuberculosis presents a complex and heterogeneous epidemiological profile, influenced by socioeconomic inequalities, population density, inadequate sanitation, and regional disparities. These factors result in distinct transmission patterns across municipalities, making local analyses essential for understanding the disease dynamics.

This project applies spatial epidemiology techniques to investigate the geographic distribution of tuberculosis in the state of Rio de Janeiro. Using GeoDa and R, spatial autocorrelation and the Barrier Index were employed to identify clusters, spatial barriers, and high-risk municipalities. The results provide insights that may support epidemiological surveillance and contribute to evidence-based public health decision-making.
